## 0. The verdict, in one line

**We already made this exact conversion once, on 2026-06-13, for the spoken channel — and wrote down why.** From `cosa-voice-integration.md:110`:

> *"word-count limits ('≤80 words', '≤120 words') failed repeatedly in practice because LLMs do not reliably count words; LLMs count sentences reliably."*

So this is not a new idea being tried. It is a ruling that has been live on one surface for two months, being extended to the surface where the money is: peer DMs.

## 2. The word that has to be defined, or the whole thing is guesswork

A limit nobody can count is not a limit. **The counting rule already exists in code and it is one sentence long** (`lupin/src/cosa/agents/dm_tutor/sentences.py`, written 2026-08-11):

> **A sentence is a unit that carries a claim. Anything that asserts nothing is structure — it is not counted.**

Which settles the cases that would otherwise be argued one at a time: a table row, a heading, a fenced code block, a horizontal rule and a bare path are **structure** and cost nothing; a bullet with prose in it **is a claim** and costs one.

**This plain-English rule goes into `brevity-mandate.md` verbatim.** Not a pointer to the module — the rule itself, so a human and an agent count the same way the gate does. Two properties make it worth stating out loud:

1. **It is why a path is free.** The 2026-08-11 "three sentences and a path" plan had to argue the path exemption as a special case. Under the claim rule it stops being an exception: a pointer asserts nothing, so it was never one of the three.
2. **It removes the obvious dodge.** Under a word count, moving prose into a table saved words. Under a naive sentence count, moving prose into a bulleted list would hide claims. Under the claim rule, a bullet that says something still costs one — so reformatting buys nothing.

⚠️ **There are two sentence counters in lupin right now and they disagree.** `dm.py:_count_sentences` (the audit) splits on `.!?` and counts table rows and headings; `dm_tutor/sentences.py` throws structure away, and the stored corpus rows carry the **naive** number.

**Measured before it could matter** (Mr Radio, 2026-08-12): only **55 of 2,951 DMs (1.9%)** change sides of the 4-claim line depending on which counter you use, and the naive counter inflates by **1.02× overall — 1.01× in the 250+ band.** I predicted the divergence would be *widest* there, since 42% of DMs over 250 words carry a list or table; it is the narrowest.

⇒ **Name a canonical counter anyway — recommend the claim counter.** A threshold with two answers is an argument waiting to happen, and the audit counter should either switch over or be labelled as a different measurement. **But no number in this plan moves either way.**

## 3. The two bars — 3 stated, rewrite above 4 — and why the gap is not a second limit

Rick's numbers: **stated limit 3 sentences; the tutor only rewrites DMs longer than 4.**

**At a trigger of 4, the tutor is not an exception handler — it runs on nearly all peer traffic** (Mr Radio, measured over 2,951 DMs, 2026-08-12):

| | |
|---|---|
| `>4 claims` fires on | **79%** (2,328 of 2,951) |
| median DM | **7.8 claims** |

⇒ **This is not a narrow gate. It is "rewrite almost everything, skip the short ones."** The one-sentence gap buys a 21% skip; the population it fires on is not near-compliant, it is more than double the stated bar.

**So the first question is cost, not disclosure.** A model call in front of four DMs in five is **the same economics that killed arm 4** — 7.9% of its projection, ~49 minutes/day of added latency. What Rick should answer first is *"can we afford a model call on 79% of DMs, and what does the skip actually save?"* — and the answer is a dial, not a yes/no (§3.1.1).

> **Superseded, kept for the record**: my first draft argued the 3-vs-4 gap was a *dead zone* where the tutor sits out on near-compliant messages, so a rewrite would cost more than it returns. That reasoning is sound and the population is wrong — at a median of 7.8 claims there is almost no near-compliant traffic for it to describe.

### 3.1.1 The trigger height is a dial, and the curve is graceful (Mr Radio's sweep, 2026-08-12)

| trigger | fires on | saving kept | words saved per model call |
|---|---|---|---|
| rewrite all | 100% | 100% | 75 |
| `>3` | 90.6% | 98.3% | 81 |
| `>4` | 78.9% | 94.4% | 89 |
| `>5` | 65.4% | 87.1% | 99 |
| `>6` | 55.4% | 79.9% | 108 |
| `>7` | **45.7%** | **70.8%** | **116** |
| `>8` | 36.6% | 61.1% | 125 |
| `>10` | 21.8% | 42.0% | 144 |

**Cost falls faster than benefit across the whole range, and each call gets more productive as the bar rises** (89 → 116 words saved per call from `>4` to `>7`). At `>7` the tutor runs on fewer than half the messages and still keeps 71% of the available saving.

**Not part of the dial**: the ≤3-claim tier — messages already at or under the stated bar — is **3.5% of all DM words and is never rewritten at any height**. It is a constant, not a lever, and should not be read as headroom.

⇒ **This is the dial, and it is Rick's to set.** Mr Radio is putting the table in front of him without recommending a height, which is right — the trade is a judgment about latency and spend, not a measurement.

### 🔴 3.1.2 The coupling nobody has named: raising the trigger re-opens the disclosure question

**§3.2 argues the disclosure call is cheap because the gap is one sentence (3 stated, 4 tolerated — 1.33×). That argument weakens as the height rises.** At `>7` the gap is 3 stated against 7 tolerated — **2.33×, close to the 2.5× of the 60-against-150 gap Rick's 2026-08-06 non-disclosure ruling was actually about.**

| chosen trigger | stated : highest tolerated | how much the disclosure call matters |
|---|---|---|
| `>4` | 3 : 4 — 1.33× | little — §3.2 holds |
| `>7` | 3 : 7 — **2.33×** | **a lot — the 08-06 precedent applies at nearly full strength** |

*(Corrected by Mr Radio: `>N` leaves an N-claim message untouched, so the highest tolerated count is N, not N+1. My first version read 3:5 and 3:8 and disagreed with the 1.33× I had used correctly elsewhere.)*

⇒ **Questions 1 and 4 in §10 are not independent. Rule the height first; the disclosure answer follows from it.** And name the second consequence out loud: at `>7` nothing rewrites a 5-sentence DM, so **the stated bar of 3 and the gate that enforces anything drift two sentences apart** — the judge still grades those messages, but the tutor is silent on them.

### 3.1.3 How big that silent band is — and why it is ONE cost, not two

Mr Radio measured the band that sits above the stated bar but below the trigger:

| trigger | messages in the silent band | share of ALL DM words |
|---|---|---|
| `>4` | 345 | **6.4%** |
| `>7` | 1,325 | **33.2%** |

At `>7`, **a third of all fleet DM words sit in a band nothing rewrites.**

⚠️ **But this is the same fact as "saving kept," not a second cost to add on top of it.** Put the two side by side:

| trigger | saving forgone (100% − saving kept) | words left unpoliced |
|---|---|---|
| `>4` | 5.6% | 6.4% |
| `>7` | 29.2% | 33.2% |

**One column, not two.** Rick should not be shown these as separate penalties — **the trade is one-dimensional: model calls saved against saving forgone**, and "a third of the words go unpoliced" is the vivid way to say "29% of the saving is left on the table."

#### 🔴 My first reading of the gap was backwards (corrected by Mr Radio, measured, 2026-08-12)

I read the two columns tracking each other and concluded the skipped band was **about as compressible as the corpus**, and therefore that "the ones we skip were mostly fine anyway" was dead. **I inferred that from the correlation instead of measuring it, and the measurement says the opposite:**

| | compressibility |
|---|---|
| skipped band at `>4` | **37%** |
| skipped band at `>8` | 53% |
| corpus average | **61%** |

**The skipped band is LESS compressible than the corpus at every height.** That is precisely why forgone saving sits *below* the unpoliced word share in every row above — one number is weighted by how much can actually come out, the other is raw words.

⇒ **"The ones we skip were mostly fine anyway" survives — weakly.** There is genuinely less to take out of them, and that argument gets *stronger* as the bar rises (37% → 53%), which is a mild point in favour of a higher trigger rather than against it.

### 3.2 Disclosure — hold the number, and how much that matters depends on §3.1.1

**Recommendation: hold it.** Rick's 2026-08-06 ruling on the DM judge is the precedent — *the grader must not divulge the limit, or senders write to 149 instead of 60* — and a published trigger invites everyone to write to it, which makes the stated bar of three decorative.

**Two things bound how much this call is worth arguing about:**

1. **The height sets the stakes** (§3.1.2). At a trigger of `>4` the stated-to-enforced gap is 1.33× and the precedent barely applies; at `>7` it is 2.7×, the same shape as the 60-against-150 gap the precedent was actually about.
2. **Our own data says the lever is weak either way.** Arm B moved the whole sample by **+73 tokens on 51,854** when the model was told its exact per-message target (2026-08-07). So both the fear (senders anchor at the trigger) and the hope (publishing it pulls the median down from 7.8) are thinly supported.

⇒ **Cheap to hold, so hold it — but rule the height first and this answer falls out.**

### 🔴 3.3 The tutor's own output is the published limit, whatever the config says

**Measured (Mr Radio, 191 delivered rewrites, 2026-08-12): only 59% land at or under three claims. 60 of the 78 misses are exactly four.** Mean output is 3.6 claims / 50 words, near-constant regardless of input.

**That defeats non-disclosure by demonstration.** `sentences.py` states the goal in its own comments — agents **mirror** the tutor's format, and that is the whole point of it. So the number the fleet learns is not the one in `lupin-app.ini`; it is **the one the rewrites visibly land on**, which today is four. Holding the trigger number private (§3.2) protects a secret the output announces four times in ten.

⇒ **Recommendation: gate the tutor's output before it sends.** A rewrite that comes back over the stated bar is re-asked **once**, then trimmed or failed closed. Same verify-tier shape as the arm-4 freeze protocol, which caught two failure classes that would otherwise have shipped.

#### 3.3.1 The gate is not free, and its price is a third argument for a higher trigger

**The *decision* costs no model call — the claim counter is deterministic. The *redo* does**, and at a 41% miss rate that is one extra call for every two and a half rewrites (Mr Radio priced it after I called the gate free):

**The gate adds a flat 41% to whatever the trigger fires on — at every height.** One re-ask per miss, and the miss rate does not vary with the trigger:

| trigger | rewrites | + re-asks at 41% | gated total |
|---|---|---|---|
| `>4` | 2,328 | 954 | 3,278 |
| `>5` | 1,931 | 792 | 2,719 |
| `>6` | 1,634 | 670 | 2,301 |
| `>7` | 1,348 | 553 | 1,898 |

⇒ **The gate does not constrain the height.** A higher trigger makes it cheaper in absolute calls, exactly as it makes everything cheaper — there is no crossover in the gate's own economics and no height at which it stops paying.

> 🔴 **Withdrawn: an earlier version of this section said `>4` "cannot afford the gate" and that `>5` was therefore a floor.** That reading compared the gated totals against a 2,951-call baseline — the cost of rewriting *every* DM once with no trigger at all, **a policy nobody has proposed.** Measured against a strawman, `>4` looks 11% over budget; measured against itself it is simply +41%, like every other height. **The floor does not exist. It was in front of Rick for roughly twenty minutes and is retracted here and in his notifications.** (Caught by Mr Radio, whose number it was.)

**So the height decides two things**: the model-call bill, and how much the disclosure question matters (§3.1.2). **The gate is an independent yes/no** — worth doing at whatever height Rick picks.

**And it settles a second-order problem Mr Radio names**: at a trigger of `>3`, a tutor landing on 4 **flags its own output as needing a rewrite.** A gate removes that; a lower trigger without a gate creates it.

| | Published to agents | Kept in config |
|---|---|---|
| "Write three sentences" | ✅ the stated bar | |
| "The tutor rewrites above N" | ❌ never | ✅ N lives in `lupin-app.ini` |
| What the tutor's message says | *"here it is in three"* — the **target** | never *"you were over four"* — the **trigger** |

⇒ **The tutor names the target it is rewriting toward, never the threshold that fired it.** That one sentence is the whole disclosure rule, and it is checkable in the tutor's prompt text.

## 4. Sentences do not bound length — the backstop that has to survive the conversion

One sentence can run 200 words. **A sentences-only rule is gameable by writing longer sentences**, and the failure would be invisible: every message compliant, tokens unchanged.

**Recommendation: sentences become the stated rule; a word or character ceiling stays in force as an undisclosed backstop.** Same shape as today — the sender is told a target and graded against a limit they cannot see.

**Measured (Mr Radio, 2,951 DMs, 2026-08-12) — the hole is real but thin, and his verdict is keep it:**

| | |
|---|---|
| widest DM the 4-claim bar would skip | **250 words in 4 claims** |
| longest single claim in the corpus | **200 words — once in 2,951** |
| share of ALL DM words sitting in skipped messages over 80 words | **2.8%** |
| … over 100 words | **0.9%** |
| median 3-claim DM | **48 words** (p90 76, p99 94) |

⇒ **The backstop is insurance, not load-bearing.** A 3-claim DM runs about 48 words, so the stated bar lands near today's 60-word target without anyone being told a word count. **Set the backstop above p99 (94 words) rather than at the old 150** — a ceiling three times the honest p99 is not a guard, it is decoration.

⚠️ **Do not carry the 150-word reject threshold into the new block by inertia.** A block that refuses on words while the rules ask for sentences is measuring one thing and instructing another.

## 6. What changes in how we tell each other to write DMs

The DM instruction today (`cross-session-communication.md:22`) is *"Say 3LoL — headline + two supporting sentences."* It becomes:

> **Three sentences: the headline, then at most two that support it. When the detail lives somewhere, send the path instead of the detail — a path is not a sentence.**

Three things worth naming about that wording:

1. **It leads with the count, not the acronym.** A worker reading it for the first time should not have to resolve `3LoL` to know what is being asked.
2. **The path clause is now a consequence, not an exception** (§2), so it can be stated in the same breath instead of needing its own paragraph and its own defence.
3. **Nothing about register changes.** WaHH is untouched — plain English in every DM, still the hardest-hit rule on this channel. Sentence counting is a length rule; it has no opinion about jargon, and three jargon-dense sentences are still a violation.

**Live receipt this is worth having**: on 2026-08-11 my own handoff DM was refused as too long, and the rewrite that passed was exactly headline + two supporting lines + a path.

## 6.5 What a manager tells a spawned worker

`brevity-mandate.md:205-212` carries the minimum spawn-brief rider, and it is the surface where the conversion pays best: **a worker that never learned to write nine-sentence DMs never has to be tutored out of it.** The rider's length line changes; the rest stands:

```
BREVITY (non-negotiable): KISS · Say 3LoL · NoMC C2C · NoAA · NoDrama · WaHH.
Three sentences: headline + at most two supporting. A line is one sentence that
makes a claim — tables, headings, code blocks and paths are structure and cost
nothing. When the detail lives somewhere, send the path instead of the detail.
Verdict first, evidence second, stop. Go longer ONLY WHEN ASKED.
Detail goes in the abstract card, never in prose or speech.
WaHH: plain English in every DM — write as if a human colleague will read it.
```

**The rider states three and never mentions the tutor.** A brief that told a worker "you'll get rewritten above four" would teach four on day one, which is §3's failure arriving through the back door — and workers are the population where a first impression is the whole habit. Same edit lands in `workflow/swe-team-spin-up.md` and `swe-team-roles.md`, which carry their own copies.
