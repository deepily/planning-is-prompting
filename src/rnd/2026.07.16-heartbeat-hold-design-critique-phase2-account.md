### Q3 (loud at WRITE only, silent at read) — ⚠️ **THE RULING IS RIGHT-ISH; THE RATIONALE IS FACTUALLY WRONG, AND THE OMITTED HALF IS THE WHOLE BUG**

The stated rationale: *"A loud **reader** would spam the hook on every legacy
file."*

**I tested the cardinality claim at host truth:**

```
[hook's reader]  stop.py:1635  →  read_hold_resilient( session_id, cwd=cwd )   →  ONE file (its own)
[the only glob]  heartbeat_hold.py:473  →  base.glob( HOLD_GLOB )              →  ALL files (the JANITOR)
```

**The hook's reader never touches a legacy file.** It reads exactly one hold: the
session's own. The 41 others are invisible to it. The rationale conflates **the
janitor's 41-file glob** with **the hook's 1-file read** — two different readers
with different cardinality — and rules on the volume of the wrong one.

A loud read in the hook is not wallpaper. It is **one warning, to exactly the
session whose hold is dead, at exactly the moment it is being poked because of
it.** That is the missing feedback loop, and it is the *precise* signal that would
have surfaced B2 four weeks ago instead of tonight.

**And the deeper problem — the guard that cannot fail.** Loud-at-write-only wires
the alarm to the path that **cannot trigger it**:

- Hand-writers never call the CLI. **0 of 22 malformed files went through
  `write_hold`** — proven by fingerprint.
- ∴ write-path loudness would have caught **0 of 22**. It fires only on the
  population that is *already well-formed by construction*.
- Meanwhile read-silence keeps the 22 actual victims silent **forever** — legacy
  files never get born again, so they never trip a write-time check.

**The alarm is structurally incapable of firing on the failure mode it exists
for.** That is the "guard that cannot fail" tell, and it is the fourth instance
of this doc's own named shape.

**My recommendation**: keep loud-at-write (it's cheap and right) **and add a
scoped loud-at-read in the hook only** — one line, one session, its own file,
rate-limited to once per session per hold-mtime. Not the janitor; the janitor
should stay silent and count. That costs nothing, cannot become wallpaper (n=1
per session), and closes the loop that hid a four-week ping-storm.

### Q4 (let the janitor eat the 41; no one-shot script) — ⚠️ **RULED ON A FALSE PICTURE OF THE DATASET. THE RATIONALE IS SOUND; THE PREMISE IS NOT.**

Rick's rationale is **excellent and I want it preserved**: *"if the janitor can't
reclaim them, it isn't fixed"* — the reclamation IS the acceptance test, and a
one-shot script would hide the defect. That reasoning is right.

**But it was ruled on the belief that the 41 are cruft. 22 of them are
irreplaceable continuity records** — and Rick was handed the census that said
otherwise. Through no fault of his; **he was given `ttl_seconds null : 22` and a
provenance section swearing it was counted.**

The collision is real: **"let the janitor eat them" now reads "let the janitor eat
`note_to_my_successor`."**

**The resolution keeps both, and it turns on one distinction the ruling never
had to make**: the acceptance test requires the janitor to **REACH and CLASSIFY**
all 41 — it does **not** require it to **DELETE** them.

1. **First tick runs in report/dry-run mode.** It enumerates every root,
   recurses, reads all 41, classifies each (prunable / kept-and-why), and
   **deletes nothing.** That output **is** the acceptance evidence — it proves
   reach + classification + the multi-root fix, and it is **strictly better than a
   one-shot script** because it exercises the real production code path. Rick's
   rationale is fully honored: if the janitor can't reach them, the report is
   empty and we know it isn't fixed.
2. **Triage the 22 before any sweep.** Extract the cargo to where it belongs
   (`memento_io`), then let the janitor eat the husks.
3. **Then enable deletion** and let it eat the ~19 husks on its own ticks. No
   script, ever.

This is the standing *"dry-run before destructive ops"* rule, and it is the one
place tonight where the doc's own motivating anecdote — Sam destroying two
irreplaceable records — is about to be re-enacted at eleven times the scale, by
automation, with no human in the loop.

**If only one thing from this review reaches Rick, make it this: the Q4 ruling is
safe to keep and unsafe to execute until the 22 are triaged.**

I found mine in the glow of an accepted finding — exactly where the standing rule

**Three pulls toward the rubber-stamp, and only one is the famous one:**
1. **The confession** (§3 self-corrects → reads as the most trustworthy section

   earn.**
2. **The receipt that isn't one** — *"the receipt block is a real code quote,

   signature line is the one place the bug is invisible."*
3. **The census — the one nobody warned me about, and the one that mattered.** A
   trap-list makes you scrutinize the named traps; **a counted table makes you
   skip the counting.** Confident numbers de-scrutinize harder than confident
   prose: prose invites argument, **a number looks like it already had one.**

**Did the two-phase ordering help, or was it theater?** It helped — **but not
where it was aimed.** It defended me against her *list*; the trap I actually hit
was her *census*, and blindness to §6 did nothing about that. **Recounting did.**
The ordering is real and it is **not sufficient**. The 19-second margin proves the
mechanism was load-bearing; the diff proves it was pointed at the wrong half of
the document.

**Proposed amendment to the tier**: the blind phase should require the reviewer
to **re-derive every counted claim** before reading any of the author's prose
conclusions. Withholding the unknowns is half the protocol. **The knowns are where
all three rounds died, and nothing currently protects them.**
