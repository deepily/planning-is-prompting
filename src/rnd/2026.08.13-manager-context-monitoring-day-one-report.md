## Commentary — how it went

### The policy's weaknesses were all found by USE. None by review.

This is the finding of the day and it is worth more than the policy itself. The document had been
read by three managers and installed in two global configuration files before a single one of its
nine corrections surfaced. **Then all nine surfaced within about an hour of anyone actually
depending on it.**

Reading a procedure tells you whether it is coherent. Only running it tells you whether it is true.

### The corrections were not typos — several reversed the rule

- **"Rows transfer, seats do not."** The original text said hand your board to a peer. Doing that
  leaves live worker sessions attached to a manager who cannot re-spin them: the board *reads* as
  covered and is not.
- **Omitting `respin_personas` silently moves the worker's rows onto the reaping manager.** The lane
  then reads as unworked while your board looks fuller. Self-concealing, which is why it is called
  out in the policy rather than left in a tool docstring.
- **"Announcing is not a control."** A manager who announces its ceiling and keeps working has
  changed nothing except who feels informed. The handoff is the control.
- **A persona-less session is invisible to the entire policy** — it does not appear where the tick
  looks, so it is never monitored and never re-spun.
- **The timer must outlive the session.** An in-session scheduler dies at exactly the moment it was
  meant to matter. On day one, two of three managers sat idle ~35 minutes while over or near the
  line, and **the one who complied was the one who had built a real crontab.**

### What I got wrong myself

- I told Krishna his memento write had not landed, having read *lupin's* slot when his seat was in the
  plan repo. He was right; I corrected it to him and to Tiffany before she spawned.
- At handoff, three plausible Krishna mementos existed and Tiffany was about to seed from the wrong
  one. **Only the freshest carried his last commit.** Verification at handoff time — not staging a
  path earlier — is what caught it.
- My own in-session timer died with my `/clear`, which is §1b of my own policy failing for its author.
  **Tiffany's durable crontab is the only timer that survived the night.**

### The thing that worked best was the cheapest

**Precommitting a trigger number while you still have room to set it well.** Cheech precommitted 88%;
I precommitted 80% and then executed at 66% because the seats were the deadline-bound part and my own
rows were not. Deciding *"at N I fire, whatever I am mid-way through"* removes the judgment call from
the moment you are least able to make it.

## The manager self-re-spin question, answered properly

**Three paths, and why two fail:**

| path | outcome |
|---|---|
| `dismiss_sessions` on your own seat | **Fails.** It reaps only sessions you spawned; your own seat is not in your lineage, and nothing of yours runs after the kill. |
| Spawn a successor, then die | **Fails.** The persona chain cannot grant your name while you hold it — the successor boots as somebody else and persona continuity breaks. |
| `/clear` in place | **Would work.** Same seat, same persona, context to zero, rehydrate from the repo-root memento. No session can type `/clear` into its own pane. |

**So the mechanical limit is real.** What closed tonight is that it does not matter as much as it
looked, because the *timing* rule routes around it: a manager spawns its successor before the wall
and hands over. The chain is self-sustaining. The orphan case bites exactly one manager — the one
that hits the wall having spawned nothing — and **that is a deadline, which is met by precommitting
a number.**

**Still worth building, and it is lupin's surface not this workflow's**: a small host-side helper that
sends `/clear` into a named pane. That would make manager self-re-spin genuinely self-service and
remove the deadline rather than asking a tiring session to judge it correctly.
