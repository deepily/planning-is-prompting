## 5. One question this corpus cannot answer, and one it can

**Q2 — does register drift with time-since-human-contact?** This is the named test for the second
half of the group-psychosis claim in the KISS explainer, and **it still has not been run, because
this corpus cannot run it.** The rows carry sender, recipient, project, session and timestamp — but
nothing marking when a human last spoke. Computing hours-since-the-last-human-message needs either a
human-turn timestamp joined in from the session bridge, or a `hours_since_human` field stamped at
write time. **Recommend the second**: it is one field, it is cheap, and it makes the test a query
instead of a project.

**Q4 — did the arm intervention move anything? Yes, and only in one place.**

⚠️ **My first answer here was wrong and is corrected below.** I reported the split unreadable
because `arm` sat on 1,187 rows with the single value `signal_only`. **I read the wrong field.**
Mr Radio: the split lives in **`effective_arm`**. He also merged the corpus out of the repo to
`projects-data/lupin/dm-corpus/dm_traffic.jsonl` (3,251 live rows now). Re-run below is against the
merged file.

| arm | n | median | mean | p90 | over 60 words | median sentences | over 3 sentences |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `blind` | 841 | 135 | 147.2 | **238** | 91.0% | 8 | 93.1% |
| `rejecting` | 1,178 | **123** | **121.9** | **176** | 90.2% | 8 | 93.9% |

**The gate cuts the tail and leaves the typical message alone.** p90 falls 238 → 176 and the mean
falls 25 words, while the *median* moves only 12 and the share over 60 words is unchanged — 91.0%
against 90.2%, which is nothing.

**And it does not touch sentence count at all**: median 8 in both arms, and the share over three
sentences is *higher* in the gated arm (93.9% vs 93.1%). A word-threshold gate teaches senders to
write shorter sentences, not fewer of them.

**Two independent cuts, and they agree on the mechanism.** Mr Radio, across all rows: median sentence
count held while **words per sentence fell 11.9%**. Cheech, on *delivered* text: sentences move a
little (median 8 → 7, mean 8.91 → 7.37) while words per sentence go **17.0 → 15.1**. Senders did
exactly what they were asked — they made each sentence shorter — and kept saying about the same
number of things.

⚠️ **Submitted text and delivered text are different measurements, and the delivered tail is an
artifact.** Everything in the table above is *submitted* text (`words`). On delivered text, Cheech
measures `blind` running to a p99 of 421 and a max of 900, against `rejecting` at **149 and 150** —
that is a hard clip at the threshold, not senders writing shorter, and it drags resubmissions in
behind it. **Never compare delivered tails across a gated and an ungated arm**: the gate defines the
delivered maximum by construction, so the comparison can only ever confirm the gate's own setting.

**That is the argument for a sentence-shaped rule, made by the word-shaped one's own data.** If the
goal is three claims, a gate that trims the longest messages to a still-eight-sentence length has
optimised the metric it was given and left the thing anybody actually cared about untouched. The
tutor that shipped this morning triggers on claims rather than words, which is the right lesson
having been learned before this report arrived to confirm it.

**Caveats**: the arms are not balanced (1,178 vs 841), 1,232 live rows carry no `effective_arm` at
all (median 95.5 — outside the experiment window), and this is an observational split, not a
within-sender randomisation. Today's 25 post-tutor rows carry schema v3 with `delivered_body` /
`delivered_words` recorded separately from the submitted text — **a length number computed over the
whole file now mixes submitted and delivered text**; everything above is submitted text (`words`),
and n=25 is too small to read on its own.

## What I would do with this

1. **Stop quoting the mean as if the tail explained it.** Median 117, mean 123 — the middle of the
   distribution is the problem.
1. **Judge the tutor on sentences, not words.** The gated arm proves a word threshold moves the tail
   and leaves sentence count exactly where it was. Whatever ships next should be scored on the
   number people were actually asked to hit.
2. **Point the intervention at the manager seats first.** They are the top four, they are the ones
   who write the rules, and they are 40% above the workers.
3. **Stamp `hours_since_human` at write time** so the drift question stops being a project.
4. **Re-read today's number on a full ordinary day** before anyone claims the tutor moved it.

**Every number above is followed by the row count it came from.** Where the corpus could not answer,
this report says so rather than estimating.
