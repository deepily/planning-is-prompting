## Why this subject

Rick's requirement was a plan small enough to cascade in five minutes that still contains **at least
one genuine design fork**. A plan too trivial to review means the reviewers find nothing and the room
watches a pipeline agree with itself — which leaves the quarter's central claim unproven in the one
place it could have been proven live.

The one-room text adventure carries that fork naturally: **parse the words, or route intent through a
model.** It isn't planted as bait; it's the actual decision anyone building this would have to make,
and both options are genuinely defensible.

**The callback.** That fork is the same shape as a claim already made in Quarter 1. If the reviewers
reach it independently, the audience watches a principle they were told about an hour earlier get
re-derived by something that wasn't in the room when it was stated. That's the moment worth having —
and it only works if nobody stages it.

## What the plan actually contains, for your own tracking

Written as a real plan, not a strawman. It carries several things a good reviewer should find, and I
am deliberately not ranking them so the cascade's ordering stays its own:

| # | In the plan | Why it's there |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | The parse-vs-model fork, with a recommendation and a stated reason | The centerpiece. Both options are argued honestly. |
| 2 | "How do we test a parser that isn't deterministic?" — named, unanswered | A real hole I could not close. If a reviewer closes it, that's a genuine save on stage. |
| 3 | Build steps 1–2 declared independent of the fork | True, and it means the decision doesn't block the build. A reviewer may or may not notice that this defuses the fork's urgency. |
| 4 | A red-herring window flagged as a quit risk | Design risk, not an engineering one. Tests whether the reviewers stay in scope. |
| 5 | Option B's per-move latency on trivial inputs like "look" | Raised but not resolved. |
| 6 | A "what I'm not sure about" section questioning the premise | Whether one room is a game or a demo of a game. |

**If the cascade finds only #1**, the run is thin but not a failure. **If it finds nothing**, the
fallback below applies.

---

## Fallbacks

- **If the cascade stalls or a reviewer seat doesn't come up**: the plan reads fine as a static
  artifact. Walk the fork verbally and show the written recommendation — the claim survives without
  the live run, it just lands weaker.
- **If reviewers converge too fast** (everyone approves in one pass): that is itself the result. Say
  so plainly rather than prompting for objections — a manufactured finding is worse than a short
  segment.
- **Do not seed a reviewer.** If the room later learns the finding was planted, every other finding
  we have shown all quarter gets discounted with it.
