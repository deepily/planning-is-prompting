## Part 3 — what the shape actually points at

**Reachability, not competence.** That distinction is the reason this record is worth keeping.

Nobody involved was careless in the ordinary sense. Every one of A–D was **measured, not guessed** — real
functions, real output, numbers anyone can reproduce. Chloé's reading was correct. Tiberius's reading was
correct. Rio's cite was correct. The measurements were all sound.

What was missing each time was one question, asked about the **input** rather than the code:

> ### Can the system actually produce the input I am measuring with?

A control that goes red on an input the system cannot emit guards nothing — the exact mirror of the more familiar
"a control that goes green on unfixed code guards nothing." Both failures feel like evidence. Both produce a
number. Only the second one is widely watched for.

And the failure **moves down a layer each round rather than resolving**:

1. Round 1 — verified the consumer (`build_paired_verdict`); did not verify the metrics dict could exist.
2. Round 2 — verified the aggregators (`compute_metrics`); did not verify which line defines `ok`.
3. Round 3 — verified the record builders; did not verify the completion metadata could carry `error`.

Each round someone checked one layer deeper and stopped one layer short. That is the observation with teeth:
**the error is not "somebody forgot to check" — it is that "I checked" always felt true, at whatever depth the
checking stopped.**
