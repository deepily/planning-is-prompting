## 0. The verdict

**The tutor arm must be VISIBLE to the sender, and that is not a design preference — it is the only lever we control.**

His §4 is the constraint everything here bends around: the interesting endpoint (does exposure teach people to write shorter?) needs **~211 triples for a 10-word shift — five to fifteen months.** Slots are not available in that quantity. **The only other term in the equation is effect size**, and effect size is set by how strongly the treatment reaches the sender.

⇒ **A silent tutor arm is an underpowered experiment made weaker on purpose.** If the sender never learns their message was rewritten, the teaching channel is *"something felt different"* — and a null result would tell us nothing about the tutor, only that invisible interventions do not teach.

## 1. His question 1 — does the sender see anything?

**Three designs, and they are genuinely different products, not framings of one.**

| | what happens | teaching channel | latency | consent |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Silent** | rewrite goes out; sender never told | ~none | none added | 🔴 **a message goes out in your name that you never saw** |
| **Disclosed-after** ⭐ | rewrite goes out; sender is shown what went out | **present, no action needed** | none added to delivery | the sender learns immediately what was sent for them |
| **Offer** | message refused; rewrite handed back; sender resends | strongest | **a full round trip** | sender approves every word |

### Recommendation: **disclosed-after**

**Why not silent**: it fails the experiment (§0) and it fails the sender. I raised the consent half on 2026-08-11 before any of this was measured — *"the sender never saw what went out in their name"* — and nothing since has softened it. A tool that rewrites your words to a colleague without telling you is not a writing teacher; it is a ghostwriter you did not hire.

**Why not offer**: it is the strongest teaching channel and it costs a round trip on **55% of all DMs** at Rick's `>6` height. That is the arm-4 latency shape again — the thing his height ruling was chosen to avoid. It also changes delivery semantics mid-experiment: an offer arm can *fail to deliver at all* if the sender never resends, which contaminates the delivered-words endpoint with an abandonment term.

**Why disclosed-after wins**: it is the only design that keeps the cost saving whole (the rewrite ships, no round trip, no abandonment) **while creating a real teaching channel**. It is also the only one where the sender can catch a bad rewrite, which is the faithfulness gap neither of our analyses closes.

⚠️ **The obvious objection, answered**: *"disclosure makes the arms distinguishable to the population being measured."* True, and here it is the point rather than a flaw — **in a teaching experiment the treatment must be perceptible.** Blinding the treatment measures the wrong thing. This is not a drug trial where perception is a placebo confound; perception is the mechanism under test.

### 1.1 The price of visibility, named — and a way to test it rather than concede it

**Mr Radio named the cost of my own recommendation rather than letting it ride** (2026-08-12): if the population can detect the treatment, a movement in submitted length may be *"responded to being rewritten"* rather than *"learned to write shorter."* That is a real limit on what the exploratory endpoint can claim.

**It is also separable, and with data his §2 schema already collects.** The two explanations predict different shapes:

| | **reaction** — responded to being rewritten | **learning** — internalised the bar |
|---|---|---|
| where it shows | the next message or two **after** a rewrite | across **all** the sender's messages |
| on messages that never fired the tutor | **no effect** | **effect present** |
| over time | **decays** between exposures | **persists** |

⇒ **The discriminator is: what happens to the messages the tutor never touched?** Those rows are in the corpus already — `submitted_words`, `submitted_sentences` and `tutor_fired`, ordered by timestamp within a sender. **No extra schema, no extra slots.**

#### Measure the SHARE, not the mean (Mr Radio's correction, simulated on the real distribution)

**My first form of this asked whether the untouched messages get shorter. That set is selected on the very thing being measured**, so learning moves messages *into* it from above and shifts its composition as well as its contents — a truncated mean, pushed back up by its own new members.

**He expected that to mask the signal; simulation says it attenuates rather than reverses**: a true 1-claim shift shows up as a **0.54-claim drop — 54% of the truth**. That halves an already-underpowered endpoint and **roughly quadruples his 211-triple figure.**

**So the statistic is the share, with the mean kept beside it:**

| statistic | why |
|---|---|
| **share of a sender's messages going untouched** ⭐ | moves **~9 points per claim**; it is a proportion, not a truncated mean, so nothing is selected away |
| mean claims of untouched messages | kept as a **consistency check** — it should move the same direction, at roughly half the size |

**One thing I checked before accepting it**, because a share of messages below the trigger obviously depends on the trigger height: **a mid-block height change injects a step into both arms at once, and the between-arm contrast cancels it.** So the share is safe in the contrast form. ⚠️ **It is not safe in any raw share we report alongside** — which turns his §7 height-freeze from a tidiness recommendation into a requirement for anything quoted outside the contrast.

**Recording it as a limit, not a solution**: if the answer is "reaction only," the tutor is still worth running for its word saving — it just is not a teacher, which is the same conclusion this morning's *cost tool, not compliance tool* finding already reached from the other direction.

## 2. His question 2 — what the tutor's message may say

Rick held the trigger number private (ruling 3, 2026-08-12). That constrains the wording, and the constraint is narrower than "don't print the number."

### 2.1 The rule

> **The tutor names the TARGET it wrote toward. It never names the TRIGGER that fired it.**

| ✅ may say | ❌ must never say |
|---|---|
| *"Here it is in three sentences:"* + the rewrite | *"Your message was 7 sentences; the limit is 6"* |
| *"Rewritten for length — this is what went out."* | *"You were 2 over"* |
| the rewrite itself, in full | any count, difference, or ratio involving the sender's own message |
| that it fired, and that length was the reason | the height, in any form — words, digits, or arithmetic |

### 2.2 🔴 The leak that is not the number — two facts that multiply

**Showing the sender their own claim count is safe. Showing it only when the tutor fires is not.**

If the sender sees *"your message: 7 claims"* in a notice that appears **only** above the trigger, then the smallest count they ever see is the trigger plus one. **Two innocuous facts multiply into the number**, and no digit was ever printed.

**This exact failure already happened**: the DM judge leaked its own enforced ceiling by arithmetic in June, through a repeated length face, and Rick fixed it by decoupling the display interval from the enforced ceiling.

⇒ **Checkable rule for the implementation session**: *the tutor's notice must not contain any measurement of the sender's message.* Not the claim count, not the word count, not a percentage. The rewrite speaks for itself.

### 2.3 What this means for `tutor_fired` being inferable

Mr Radio asks whether the arms stay distinguishable to the population. **Under disclosure, yes — deliberately**: the sender knows the tutor fired, because that is the treatment. **What stays hidden is the height**, which is what Rick's ruling protects. Those are different secrets and only the second one was ruled.

## 3. His question 3 — reporting language for a fourth block

This block follows **two data-dependent extensions**, both made by someone who had seen the interim numbers (`generate_schedule.py:88-96`). The existing rule carries forward unchanged: **fix the end date before the first slot opens, report blocks separately as well as pooled, and say so wherever a p-value appears.**

**Three additions this block needs, all because of its own shape:**

1. **Each endpoint carries its status in the block record, not in a doc.** Mr Radio's Option B declares two endpoints with different standing — *delivered words* is confirmatory of a mechanical claim, *submitted words* is exploratory and underpowered by design. **Written into the block itself**, so a later reader cannot pick up the exploratory number and quote it as a finding. A pre-registration that lives in an editable document is not a pre-registration.

2. **The delivered-words result must be labelled for what it is.** The tutor rewrites to ~51 words against a 60-word target, so the analyzer's excess metric collapses **by construction**. Reporting that as "the tutor significantly reduced DM length" would be true and misleading in the same sentence. Suggested standing phrasing: *"confirms in production a mechanical result already measured offline at n=200; not evidence about behaviour."*

3. **The block records its frozen trigger height.** Rick's ruling made the height runtime-configurable, so a mid-block change would silently make the block two conditions. Mr Radio flags this in his §7 and I agree it belongs beside the seed — **and it needs one thing more: a recorded value the analyzer reads, not a config the analyzer trusts to have been stable.**
