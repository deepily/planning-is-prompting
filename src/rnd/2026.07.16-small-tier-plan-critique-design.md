## 3. WHY IT IS NEARLY FREE — cost-order, not drama-order

The **Reviewer seat already exists** in the standing crew, and **design critique is already its
mandate**:

> `swe-team-roles.md:91` — *"**Mandate** — Adversarial design + code review. You try to **refute**,
> not rubber-stamp."*
> `swe-team-roles.md:92` — knows on arrival: *"**The spec** + the implementer's diff."*

**Nothing new is created.** No role, no spawn, no seat, no second doc surface. An existing seat's
existing duty moves **one phase earlier**.

### 3.1 The gap this closes is SEQUENCING, not authority

The gate flow (`swe-team-roles.md:29`) is **implement → review → test**:

    implementer reports done + self-tested
      → adversarial reviewer renders refute-first verdict
      → tester posts integration green
      → Manager verifies both → commits

**There is no named step where the reviewer attacks the spec BEFORE code exists.** The reviewer
*may* review design — it's in the mandate — but the flow only ever hands them a design *with a diff
already attached to it*. By then the design's cost is sunk and the artifact in front of them is the
diff. **The mandate says design; the sequencing delivers code.**

This is the whole amendment: **one phase boundary.**

### 3.2 A partial answer to R-2's open question (claimed modestly)

The M1 post-game flagged an open design question I called *arguably worth more than the whole R-1
build*: **"what forces the listening?"** A deposit is cheap; **nothing compels anyone to read it**
(Sam's refutation of R-2, adopted: Cheech's 14:13:49 deposit produced F-EDUPE only because Tiberius
*happened* to be reading the topic).

A pre-implementation critique **forces exactly one act of listening** — the implementer cannot start
until the critique lands. That is **one** compelled read, not a general solution.

> ⚠️ **Do not overclaim this.** It forces the reviewer to READ the design. It does **not** force the
> author to *heed* the critique, and it does nothing for the deposit-listening problem at large.
> R-2 stays open. Recorded here so a later reader doesn't mistake this for its closure.

## 4. THE MINIMUM CONTRACT — the part Rick ruled canonizable

### 4.1 The slot

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| **Name** | ⏸️ **PENDING RICK** |
| **Fires on** | Pattern 3 work, and any bug fix carrying a design doc, where an other-seat critique is affordable |
| **Fires when** | **BEFORE implementation begins** — the phase boundary is the feature |
| **Run by** | the **Reviewer seat** (existing crew role), never the design's author |
| **Tracked in** | history.md (per the Pattern 3 row of the matrix) — **not** a new doc surface |

### 4.2 🔑 THE BAR — this IS the feature

Without a falsifiable bar this degrades into *"the reviewer glanced at it and said it looks good"* —
**a check that cannot fail**, which is the exact object the heartbeat-hold design is about, and the
exact object R-3 names: *"a measurement that cannot distinguish the claim from its negation."*
Shipping an unfalsifiable critique step would be this workflow's own disease, canonized.

> **CONTRACT — the critique MUST produce one of:**
> **(a)** a **refutation of ≥1 specific claim** in the design, cited to the claim; **or**
> **(b)** a **written account of what was attacked and survived** — what they tried to break, how,
> and why it held.
>
> **NOT a verdict. AN ACCOUNT.** "Looks good" is a contract violation, not a pass.

The charter already supplies the language — this only makes it produce **evidence**:

> `swe-team-roles.md:93` — *"A clean review is one you **tried and failed** to break."*
> `swe-team-roles.md:96` — *"If you can't verify a concern, mark it as a **question**, not a
> finding."*

**Provenance rider (R-A ②, Rick-ratified 2026-07-13)** — the account names **the act and the
position**: *"I ran the janitor against a planted null-TTL hold, alone, before reading his §4"*
beats *"the design holds."* This is the field that lets the account refute a bad design **after the
reviewer is gone**.

### 4.3 ⚠️ THE RISK THE DOC MUST NAME ABOUT ITSELF

**A confident, receipt-dense design is HARDER to refute, not easier.**

> Tiberius, 2026-07-16: *"I attacked the multiset and accepted the key. The difference was that the
> key came with a **RATIONALE** and the multiset didn't… So I never asked it."*

A design armored in citations reads as **already-verified**, and the rational move for a reviewer is
to spend their scrutiny elsewhere. **This is a first-class failure mode of the tier, not a caveat.**

The heartbeat-hold design (`2026.07.16-heartbeat-hold-lifecycle-janitor-design.md`) is this tier's
**first test case and its worked example of the risk**: it is nothing but rationale, it is authored
by the Steward who wrote this contract, and its underlying bug **has already been diagnosed wrong
twice** — once by that same Steward. **If the tier cannot bite that document, the tier does not
work.**
