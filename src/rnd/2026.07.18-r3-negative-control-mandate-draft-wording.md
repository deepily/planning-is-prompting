### 1.2 ⚠️ FINDING THAT CHANGES THE PROPOSED HOME

The assignment proposes `testing-baseline.md` for the mandate. **`neutral-execution.md:134` already carries it:**

> *"A green that could not have been red, and a red that could not have been green, are **the same bug**: an instrument with one moving part missing. **Every probe needs a negative control** — one that reproduces the failure class *without* triggering it."*

Adding a second mandate in `testing-baseline.md` would create **two floors covering each other** — which is precisely the sub-class Rachel measured against María's own ruling this morning and María corrected at `1a39f9c`. The graduation would reproduce the defect it is graduating about, one layer up, in doctrine instead of code.

**Recommendation**: do not add a second mandate. **Strengthen and cross-reference the existing one**, and put the *new* material — the sub-classes, which `neutral-execution.md` does not cover — in `post-game.md` §7 as anti-patterns. One floor, load-bearing, with the catalogue of ways it fails living where retros look.

I flag that I am arguing against the home a Steward proposed. I think the evidence requires it; Rick should weigh that I may be over-applying today's lesson because today's lesson is fresh in my hands.

### 1.3 Proposed wording — amendment to `neutral-execution.md` (one line, appended to the existing rule)

> **A negative control is only a control if you have SEEN it fail.** Assert both polarities by construction: run the probe against a deliberately broken subject and capture the red as **bytes**, not as a claim that it was red. A control you have only reasoned about is a control you have not got. **Prefer one control you have watched fail to three you believe in.**

### 1.4 Proposed wording — five new anti-patterns for `post-game.md` §7

Register matched to the existing bullets: name — one-line diagnosis — founding case with receipt.

- **A redundancy that hides vacuity** — two floors covering the same failure, each green whether or not the other exists, so neither is ever observed doing work. Redundancy is a virtue in load paths and a **defect in evidence**: it guarantees you cannot tell which floor is holding. Delete one and watch what fails. *Founding case: 2026-07-18, R-1's byte-floor and line-floor masking each other; found by Rachel measuring the Steward's own ruling; corrected at `1a39f9c`.*

- **A contaminated instrument that reds out regardless** — a mutation harness leaking state between runs reds out no matter what you change, so **red carries no information**. Note the direction of the error: it inflates apparent sensitivity, which is the flattering direction, which is why it survives. *Founding case: 2026-07-18, Krishna's five-mutation loop leaking through an unquoted heredoc; every batch-derived number withdrawn. The instrument for detecting checks-that-cannot-fail **was one**.*

- **Sensitivity cited as preservation** — a mutation table answers *"do my tests fire when I break the code?"* It can never answer *"is this still the same thing?"* A refactor that silently drifted leaves the table exactly as green. **No quantity of mutation results substitutes for a diff against a recorded baseline.** *Founding case: 2026-07-18, "nothing here changes behavior" asserted over a clean sensitivity table after a function extraction; caught only because a reviewer held an outside 12-form baseline and re-ran it.*
  **This is the hardest sub-class and deserves its own note**: nothing in the artifact is wrong. The measurement is honest, isolated, correctly obtained — and cited for a claim outside what it measures. **The error lives only in the join between the claim and the evidence.** Classes above are found by examining the artifact harder; this one cannot be. It requires holding the sentence and the measurement side by side, which the author is structurally worst placed to do on their own sentence. **Discipline fixes the others; only a second seat fixes this one.**

- **A length accident wearing a PASS** — a threshold met by an artifact's incidental size rather than by the property the threshold exists to detect. The gate reports PASS and has measured nothing. *Founding cases: 2026-07-18, Clayton (700 B) and Rachel (883 B).*

- **A crash wearing a FINDING** — an instrument failing to run, reported as the subject failing the check. *Founding case: 2026-07-18, Rachel, self-retracted before it reached a verdict.*

- **⭐ A coverage gate mistaken for a correctness gate** — **coverage measures lines executed; it never measures claims verified.** A module can sit under a 100% lines-branches-functions gate and ship a semantic defect, because every line ran and no line was ever asked whether it did the right thing. *Founding case: 2026-07-18, `broadcast_handler.py` — 100% on all three axes, semantic defect shipped anyway.*
  **This is the strongest exhibit R-3 has, and the reason is that it required nobody to break anything.** Every other sub-class above is demonstrated by deliberately damaging something and observing that the check stayed green. This one was demonstrated by a defect arriving on its own, through a gate reporting perfect compliance. **A green that was never capable of being red does not need an adversary to be worthless — it only needs to be believed.**
  ⚠️ *Provenance: reported by the Steward; **I did not re-run the coverage report or reproduce the defect.** Rick should treat this exhibit as inherited unless someone re-derives it. Given that it is the strongest claim in the section, it is the one most worth a second pair of hands before it is graduated.*

## 2. `zero-self-catches` — **RECOMMEND AGAINST GRADUATING AS WORDED**

The claim: across S9 → S128 → S132 → today, every defect was found by someone other than its author.

I believe the underlying phenomenon is real. **I do not believe this claim, as worded, is supported by the method that produced it**, for three reasons — the third of which I think is disqualifying.

### 2.1 The data now comes from the seats appointed to produce it

Today's run supplies its fourth data point from six seats explicitly assigned to cross-check each other. In that configuration, "defects were found by non-authors" is **guaranteed by the process design**, not discovered by observation. Rachel named this risk about her own finding and declined to push it; the same bar applies here, and it applies to me: **I am one of the seats whose output becomes a data point in a rule I am being asked to draft.**

### 2.2 It flatters everyone who would ratify it

The rule's practical effect is *"cross-review is indispensable"* — proposed by cross-reviewers, to a Steward who runs cross-reviews, drafted by the seat most credited in this run. Every incentive points one way. That alone is not disqualifying, but it means the burden of proof should be raised, not lowered, and today's enthusiasm is the worst moment to lower it.

### 2.3 ⛔ The disqualifying problem: the claim cannot observe its own negative case

**A self-catch does not leave a record.** An author who spots their own defect fixes it silently, and nothing enters the transcript, the commons, or the post-game. The measurement can only see defects that *survived to be found by someone else*. So "zero self-catches across four runs" measures **what gets recorded**, not what gets caught.

**A claim that cannot observe its own negative case is a check that cannot fail.** Rule 2 would be graduated on the strength of exactly the defect Rule 1 exists to name. That is not an argument I can make quietly in a footnote.

**And today produced a counter-example.** My own mutation anchor went stale against Krishna's extraction; my harness hard-aborted, wrote nothing, and I reported it myself. Nobody caught it for me. Had it silently no-op'd I would have filed a false finding against his guard. **That is a self-catch, it is in today's data, and it was recorded only because I chose to write it down** — which is itself evidence for the recording bias above.

### 2.4 What I would graduate instead

The self-catch that did happen was not produced by vigilance. It was produced by a **precondition** — a mutation that could not find its target had to abort rather than silently no-op. That is the true and useful form, and it is falsifiable:

> **Authors do not reliably catch their own defects by re-reading; they catch them when a MECHANISM refuses to proceed.** Prefer a precondition that hard-aborts to an intention to be careful. A rule whose only enforcement is "remember to check" fails first on the afternoon it was written for.

This is narrower, it is supported by an instance we can point at, it does not require observing the unobservable — and it composes with the existing §7 bullet *"a rule whose only mechanism is 'remember to do this'"* rather than duplicating it.

**Recommendation**: hold `zero-self-catches` as an observation in the Decisions Log; propose the mechanism-form above to Rick as the graduation candidate instead. If he prefers the original wording, it needs a method that can observe self-catches — e.g. seats deposit near-misses, not just findings.
