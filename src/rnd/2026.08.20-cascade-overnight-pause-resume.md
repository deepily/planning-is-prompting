# Pausing a cascaded review overnight — ad-hoc instructions

**Written**: 2026-08-20, 23:05 EDT · **Author**: María 🌸 · **Requested by**: Rick, broadcast `077c463f`

> *"What's your opinion on interrupting a Cascaded Review in the middle? Do we have a workflow
> designed for pausing and then resuming a workflow like this? If not… write up an ad-hoc set of
> instructions for everyone to follow first thing in the morning."*

---

## Short answers first

**Is there a workflow for this? No.** Two things exist and neither covers it:

| what exists | what it actually covers | why it does not fit |
|---|---|---|
| `workflow/fleet-pause-resume.md` | operator pauses the **whole fleet** | never mentions cascade; it is about halting sessions, not preserving a review's position |
| cascade "user-pause" (`plan-review-cascaded.md:684`, `:800`) | a section **waits for Rick's decision** while workers sit idle | assumes the workers are **still alive**. Overnight they are not — they get re-spun or die, and their context goes with them |

**Is interrupting mid-cascade safe? Yes — on one condition.** It is safe exactly to the degree that
findings were written to **disk** as they landed, rather than left in reviewer context and DM
history. Tonight that condition held: every finding went into the plan document as its own commit,
so the review's output survives the sessions that produced it.

**What does NOT survive**: which reviewer was mid-attack on which claim, and any finding a reviewer
had formed but not yet sent. That is the real loss, and it is small — a re-read finds it again.

---

## The one rule worth keeping

> **A cascade's durable artifact is the DOCUMENT, not the sessions.** If a finding is not in the
> document, it does not survive the night. Reviewers should land findings continuously, not batch
> them for a final report.

Tonight's cascade found ~12 real defects across four passes and lost none of them, because each was
committed into the plan as it arrived. That is the whole mechanism.

---

## Morning resume — do these in order

### 1. Read the document, not the transcripts *(everyone, 5 min)*

The plan carries a **`STATE AT STOP`** section at the top: what is settled, what is open, what is
owed, and who owns each. Start there. Do not reconstruct from DM history — it is longer, partial,
and was condensed in transit.

### 2. Author (María): confirm the freeze is real *(2 min)*

```bash
git -C <worktree> status --short          # expect clean
git -C <worktree> log --oneline <base>..HEAD | wc -l
```

State the sha out loud before anyone builds on it. A "frozen" claim that nobody re-checked is how a
night's work gets built on the wrong commit.

### 3. Close the open questions BEFORE resuming the review *(Rick + author)*

Questions the reviewers cannot answer — anything that decides what the system *is* — get ruled
first. Resuming a cascade with product questions still open just generates findings nobody can act
on, which is what stopping avoided.

### 4. Reviewers resume with a WHOLE-DOCUMENT read, not more individual findings *(manager)*

🔴 **This is the part most worth carrying forward.** After a long pause the document has changed
more than any reviewer has read. Tonight: eleven commits went in while the cast was reviewing
individual claims — and **the largest defect of the night existed precisely because nobody had read
the revised sequence end to end.**

⇒ **First pass after a resume is always a full re-read.** Individual findings after that.

### 5. Re-run any measurement whose store may have moved *(manager)*

A count taken last night against a live store is a fact about last night. If anything wrote to that
store since — a test run, an eval — the number needs retaking before a decision leans on it.

---

## For the manager standing a cast back up

- **Do not try to restore the same reviewers.** Cheaper to spawn fresh readers against the current
  document than to rehydrate context that is now stale anyway. A cascade reviewer's value is a
  careful read, and that is reproducible.
- **Re-brief from the document's open-questions list**, one reviewer per open question, rather than
  re-issuing last night's assignments — those were aimed at claims that have since been revised or
  withdrawn.
- **Say explicitly what has already been ruled**, or reviewers will re-litigate settled decisions in
  good faith. Tonight's rulings are recorded in the plan with Rick's words quoted; point at them.

## For the author

- **Nothing is built during the pause** unless the user says otherwise. If some steps were cleared
  to build, say so and let the user decide — Rick's call tonight: *"build only and all of the parts
  when everything's been resolved. I'm not in a hurry — I prefer to have this done well once instead
  of fixing it seventeen times thereafter."*
- **Record what you were WRONG about, not only what was decided.** Two of tonight's best findings
  came from someone checking a claim of mine that was mistaken. A resume note that hides the author's
  errors removes the reviewers' best lead.

---

## Should this become a real workflow?

**Not yet.** One night is one data point, and the standing practice here is to let a pattern recur
across sessions before graduating it into `workflow/`. If a second cascade pauses and this document
is what gets reached for, that is the signal — then it earns a place beside
`plan-review-cascaded.md`, most likely as a section rather than a file of its own.

What would go in it, on tonight's evidence: the durable-artifact rule (§"The one rule"), the
whole-document re-read on resume (§4), and the instruction to re-take measurements (§5).
