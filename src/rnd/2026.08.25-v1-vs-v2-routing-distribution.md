# v1 vs v2 — the shape of the answers, not the threshold

**Written**: 2026-08-25 ~21:35 EDT · María 🌸 (session `8be9357f`)
**Artifacts**: v1 `lupin/io/v2-flow/paired-run-ts-f06f5961/v1-arm-artifact.json` · v2 `lupin/io/v2-flow/eval-2026-08-25-19-31-31/` (`v2-arm-artifact.json`, `records.jsonl`)
**Measured off the artifacts, not read off any report.**

---

## Why this document exists

I asked Rick to rule on how big a margin the `v2 ≥ v1` floor should carry, because one
utterance out of sixty could flip it (checklist §10b, E2 — open since 2026-08-21).

He refused the question:

> *"We are arguing about a number when we should be discussing what do the results look like?
> What is the shape of the distribution of the answers obtained? Where does it fail? On either
> version where is it always right? Where do they diverge? Where do they converge?"*

He was right. **On this data the gap is not one utterance — it is 55/60 against 21/45, on
different mixes.** A margin was the wrong instrument for a difference that size, and I had spent
three days not asking a question that turned out to be the wrong question.

---

## 1. Where they converge

Cold pass, n=100, `sample_signature ff79d7e6`, seed 1024, both arms.

| expected command | v2 chose correctly | v1 could measure at all |
|---|---|---|
| `agent router go to todo` | **20 / 20** | 20 / 20 |
| `agent router go to math` | **20 / 20** | 17 / 20 |
| `agent router go to calculator` | **15 / 20** | 8 / 20 |
| `agent router go to automatic` | 0 / 20 — all 20 → receptionist | **0 / 20** |
| `none` | 19 → receptionist ✓, 1 → math | 19 / 20 |

**`todo` and `math` are solved.** Forty utterances, forty exact matches, zero disagreement. Any
future comparison that keeps re-measuring these two is spending budget to re-confirm a settled
result.

The arithmetic reproduces the artifact exactly — eligible 60 (`calculator` + `math` + `todo`),
correct 55, **0.9167**. That is a check on my reading, not a finding.

---

## 2. Where v2 fails — three distinct things, and only one is a defect

The single number `0.9167` hides three unrelated situations. Separating them is most of the
value in this document.

### 2a. `calculator` — the only true miss, and it is already ruled

4 of 20 routed to `math`, 1 to a bare `math` (§5). This is **not new**: it is the
arithmetic-versus-algebra boundary Rick ruled on **2026-08-22** — *the router is right, relabel
the corpus*. Arithmetic belongs to the calculator; algebra and geometry to the math agent.

⇒ **The routing number is showing a corpus-labelling debt, not a router defect.** Follow-on work
is row `11af54f8` (add a simple arithmetic function to the calculator). Until the relabel lands,
this category keeps reporting misses that are not misses.

### 2b. `automatic` — a void, not an error

**20 of 20** come back `route_reason: unknown_command`. The command is not recognised at all.

§10a pin #2 excludes `automatic` from the routing denominator, and that is **correct** — an
unmappable command cannot be a routing hit or miss. But:

> ⚠️ **"Excluded" reads as *untested* on a report when it actually means *no capability exists*.**

Those are opposite findings. A reader scanning for gaps sees an excluded row and moves on. This
deserves explicit wording on the report — the same three-state treatment pins #9 and #10 gave the
cache family and the degradation rates, one family further out.

### 2c. `none` — not a failure at all

Reads as 0/20 exact-match and is **19/20 correct**, because §10a pin #2 pins
`none ≡ agent router go to receptionist`. It only looks like a failure if you match strings
naively — which is precisely why that pin exists.

---

## 3. Where they diverge — and why the delta is still unreadable

v1 measured **45 of the same 60** eligible utterances. Its 15 missing ones are **not scattered**:

| category | v1 lost | share of that category |
|---|---|---|
| `agent router go to calculator` | **12** | 60% |
| `agent router go to math` | 3 | 15% |
| `agent router go to todo` | 0 | — |

**Twelve of fifteen losses fall in `calculator` — the one category v2 also finds hardest.**

⇒ v1 is graded on a mix with the hard end removed, and still returns **21/45 = 46.7%** against
v2's **55/60 = 91.7%**. The two accuracies are not comparable, and no restatement of them is.
This is Mr Radio's category-composition finding seen from the per-category side.

**A delta across these two arms would flatter v2 twice** — once because v2 answered more of the
hard category, once because v1's missing rows are the ones it would most likely have failed.

---

## 4. What I could not compute, and why

**v1's arm artifact carries no per-utterance routing.** It holds `spans_by_utterance` (latency
only, 64 entries) and aggregate metrics. There is no per-utterance `actual_command`.

⇒ **Per-utterance agree/diverge is not computable from these artifacts.** "Where do they diverge"
can be answered at *category* level and no finer. Rick asked for utterance-level shape; the data
does not carry it, and saying so beats synthesising it.

That is the same missing-detail defect as the rest of 2026-08-25, in a new place: an artifact
that supports the number it publishes and nothing else, so every question one layer down needs a
re-run.

**What would fix it**: have v1's arm record `actual_command` per utterance the way v2 does.
Cheap, and it turns every future paired run into a real confusion matrix instead of two scalars.

---

## 5. One loose thread

Two spellings for one route appear in `payload.command`: **`math`** and
**`agent router go to math`**. Anything grouping by that string counts them as different
commands, and an exact-match check scores the bare form as a miss even when the route was right.
One record in a hundred here — which is why it would survive indefinitely.

Filed as row `759a895b` (P3, Mr Radio). Direction deliberately not guessed: it could be a second
emitter, a truncation, or a genuinely different command.

---

## 6. What this says about the E2 question

**E2 stays open, and it should be re-scoped before it is asked again.**

Asking "how big a margin" presumes the two accuracies are comparable and differ by a little. On
the only paired run we have they are **not comparable** (§3) and differ by a lot. The margin
question becomes real only after a run where both arms measure the same utterances — and at that
point the honest answer may be that no margin is needed, or that the right control is a
minimum-n per *category* rather than a global threshold.

⇒ **Do not re-ask E2 until a paired run exists whose arms measure the same set.** Re-asking it
against these numbers would produce a threshold fitted to an artifact of v1's failures.
