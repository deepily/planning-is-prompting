## 2. Why it happens — the part a rule has to survive

A rule that only says *"do the important one"* will not hold, because the pull toward P2 is structural, not lazy:

1. **A P2 is closeable in one sitting. A P0 is not.** A seat that wants a receipt takes the row that can produce one.
2. **P2s are discovered mid-P0.** You are working a P0, you trip over a broken guard, and filing-plus-fixing it feels like the same motion.
3. **Nothing ever compares what you took against what you left.** Not the store, not a doc, not a reviewer.
4. **The flow-ratio gate rewards closing anything.** Close two P3s and the gate opens. It counts rows, not value.

⇒ So the policy needs a **mechanical check**, not an exhortation. *A rule that depends on remembering is not installed.*

---

## 3. The rule — three clauses

### R1 — Take the highest priority you can actually work
> A seat takes the **highest-priority workable row it owns**. Workable = `queued`, not blocked, not parked.

### R2 — Skipping is allowed, and it must be written in the row
> A seat MAY take a lower-priority row when a higher one is not workable — blocked, awaiting a ruling, needs a peer, or genuinely bigger than the remaining context. **It must say so in the skipped row's body, in one line, before taking the other.**

Not a request for permission. A **receipt** — so *"the P0 was blocked"* and *"the P0 was unappealing"* stop printing identically.

### R3 — Discovered work is filed, not worked
> A defect found while working a P0 is **filed and left**. It is worked when it is the highest-priority workable row, or when it **blocks** the P0 — and if it blocks, say that in the row.

This is the clause that bites hardest. It is also the one that would have prevented most of tonight's 37.

---

## 4. Enforcement — where a rule stops depending on memory

**The check**: on `queued → in_progress`, the store looks for another **workable** row with the same owner at a **strictly higher** priority.

| option | behaviour | cost |
|---|---|---|
| **A. Warn** | transition succeeds, response names the row you skipped | ~10 lines. Ignorable. |
| **B. Refuse unless a reason is given** ⭐ | 422 unless `reason` is supplied, which is then stamped on the skipped row | ~40 lines + tests |
| **C. Hard refuse** | cannot take a lower row while a higher is workable | brittle — every legitimate skip becomes a fight |

**Recommended: B.** It cannot be ignored like a warning, it cannot deadlock like a hard refuse, and **the reason lands in the durable record** where the next reader sees it.

**Blast radius**: the ratchet risk is that a mis-prioritised row wedges a seat. B has the escape hatch built in — you type the reason and proceed.

## 5. What this does NOT fix

- **It does not make P0s smaller.** The pull toward closeable work survives any rule.
- **It does not touch the flow-ratio gate**, which still rewards closing anything. If closing two P3s opens the gate, the incentive is intact. *(Related, measured tonight by Rachel: `wont_fix` closes never register in that gate's count — 2 rows all-time, real but small.)*
- **It only sees rows in the store.** Work done without a row is invisible to it, as it is to every query I ran tonight.
- **It cannot tell a real skip from a plausible-sounding one.** R2 makes the reason visible, not true.
