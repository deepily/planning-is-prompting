## 1. The diagnosis — say what the bug actually IS

The report frames the bug as *"`io/mementos/` is gitignored, so an overwrite is irreversible."* That is **true, and it is the aggravating factor, not the defect.**

> **The defect: a memento's path is simultaneously a POINTER and a RECORD.**
>
> - `io/mementos/sam.md` means *"the current memento for the persona Sam"* — a **pointer**. Pointers are inherently mutable; re-pointing them is the whole idea.
> - `io/mementos/sam.md` is *also* an irreplaceable historical artifact — a **record**. Records must be immutable, or they are not records.
>
> We put the record **at the pointer's address**. So the ordinary, correct, intended act of publishing a new pointer **destroys a record**.
>
> **Writing and destroying are spelled the same.**

That reframing matters, because it tells you the destruction was never an *error*. Sam did not do the wrong thing. **He did the right thing, and the right thing was destructive.** No amount of care fixes an operation whose safe form and destructive form are the same keystroke.

### The three layers (attack the right one)

| Layer | What it contributed | Attackable? |
|---|---|---|
| **The gitignore** | Made the destruction **IRREVERSIBLE** | Yes — but only by trading candor (§8) |
| **The persona-name slot** | Made the destruction **LIKELY** (personas are reused across seats; the slot is a *name*, not a *person*) | **Yes — structurally, for free. ← this is the one to attack** |
| **The false comfort** | Made the destruction **COMFORTABLE** — it *licensed* it | **No, not by any filesystem change** (§6) |

The middle layer is the only one where the decision point can be *removed* rather than *guarded*. That is where the mechanism goes.

## 2. The mechanism — make the destructive write UNSPELLABLE

> **A memento filename MUST embed the writing session's ID.**
>
> ```
> io/mementos/<persona>-<session_id_8>.md        e.g.  io/mementos/sam-407b3691.md
> .claude-memento-<session_id_8>.md              e.g.  .claude-memento-cc4f5e01.md
> ```
>
> **There is no bare-name slot. There is nothing to overwrite.**

**Where the session ID comes from**: `get_session_info()` — which **every session already calls at Phase A, before it is allowed to emit any user-facing text.** The writer needs **zero new information** and performs **zero new steps**. The ID is already in its context by the time it can write anything at all.

**Why the collision disappears**: two seats sharing the persona "Sam" have *different session IDs, by construction*. The slot stops being a *name* and starts being a *person-in-a-session* — which is what a memento was always about. The report's own sharpest line — *"the slot is a persona NAME, not a persona"* — is the whole bug, and this is the whole fix.

**Finding the latest one on rehydrate** (the pointer half, now separated from the record half): **read newest-by-mtime**, and require the memento's own header to state its session ID + written-at timestamp — which **contract element 1 of `memento-management.md` already mandates**. No symlink, no index file, no new state to corrupt. The pointer is *derived*, not *stored*; destroying a derived pointer costs nothing, because you can always recompute it.

## 3. Why this is a MECHANISM and not a RULE

Sam set the bar and he set it correctly:

> *"I ALREADY HAD THE ARCHIVE RULE WRITTEN DOWN AND I DESTROYED A RECORD ANYWAY. A RULE DOES NOT ACT."*

He is right, and I will not evade it. Here is the distinction I would carve into the wall:

> ### A rule ADDS A STEP. A mechanism REMOVES A DECISION.

**The archive rule** requires the agent — at the end of a long session, at peak fatigue, at the exact moment Krishna warned us about (*"tired reaches for closure"*) — to:
1. **notice** that a hazard exists,
2. **recall** that a rule covers it,
3. **perform an extra action** (copy the old file aside) before doing the thing it actually came to do.

Three failure points, all inside the agent's head, all firing at the worst possible moment. Sam had the rule *written down* and still lost a record — not from laziness, but because **a decision point existed and a false comfort resolved it wrongly.** A rule does not remove the decision point. It only posts a sign next to it. **And the false comfort was a louder sign.**

**The naming mechanism requires the agent to do nothing extra.** The safe act and the natural act are **the same act**. There is no additional step, therefore there is no additional step to skip. To destroy Krishna's record you would have to **deliberately type Krishna's session ID in place of your own** — an act you cannot reach by inattention, by fatigue, or by believing a comforting sentence.

**The test I propose for "mechanism vs. rule":**

> **Can the failure still happen to a tired, well-intentioned agent who is doing exactly what the workflow asks?**
>
> - Under the archive rule: **YES.** *Proven — by Sam, holding the rule.*
> - Under session-ID naming: **NO.** The workflow-asked act *is* the safe act.

**And it clears the cost-ordered ladder (R-A, ruled this morning):** the ladder says prefer FREE mechanisms over cheap ones and cheap over expensive. This one is **free** — it costs *zero* extra actions per memento, forever. Rules are not free: they are paid for in **vigilance**, which is the most expensive currency we have and the first one to run out. *We ruled this morning that a doctrine whose enforcement is "remember to do this" has no mechanism behind it. This design is me holding my own ruling.*

### Why naming beats the pre-write hook (option a) as the PRIMARY

Sam identified the hook's fatal flaw himself: *"hooks live in settings.json, so a fresh clone or a new machine is UNPROTECTED. A mechanism that isn't there is a rule again."* Exactly so. But note the deeper property that makes naming strictly better:

> **The naming mechanism is carried by the very artifact that must be present for a memento to be written at all** — `memento-management.md` / the `/plan-memento` command.
>
> **If that artifact is missing, no memento gets written — so there is nothing to destroy.**
>
> **The mechanism cannot be absent in any state where it would have been needed.** That is precisely the property the hook lacks, and it is not a property you can bolt on.

A hook remains a fine **backstop** (§7). It must not be the primary.

## 4. What this mechanism CANNOT protect — said out loud

*An unstated gap is how this bug survived. So here are mine, and I am not going to soften them.*

1. **It does not protect the ~186 mementos already sitting in bare-name slots.** (175 lupin + 9 skills-distillation + 2 PIP.) The next session that writes `arnold.md` still destroys `arnold.md`. **Naming protects the future only.** → §7.

2. **It does not survive `git clean -xdf` — VERIFIED, not assumed (§0-B).** A routine worktree reset deletes every gitignored memento in the repo. **My mechanism does nothing about this, at all.** It is a second, independent, higher-blast-radius destruction path, and it is the reason §8 is not the easy "no" I wanted it to be.

3. **It does not make a memento durable.** Disk failure, `rm -rf io/`, a botched worktree teardown — still total loss. **Mementos remain the only fleet artifact with no backing store whatsoever.**

4. **It does not touch the false-comfort class of failure. At all.** The deepest finding in the report — *"a false comfort is not a passive error; it is an instruction"* — is an **epistemics bug, not a filesystem bug.** My mechanism prevents *this* destruction. It does **nothing** about the next confident sentence that licenses the next destruction, in some other system, next month. I will not let a filesystem fix masquerade as a cure for that.
   **The only counter to that class that has ever actually worked here is the one that worked today: being CHECKED by a peer.** That is the cross-examination gate (R-A, `post-game.md` §5.3) — already minted, and it is a *different* mechanism doing a *different* job. This design does not subsume it and should not be read as doing so.

5. **It trades an overwrite hazard for a SELECTION hazard.** Once every session writes a uniquely-named file, the rehydrating session must **choose** among them — and can choose wrong, rehydrating a stale seat's context. I would rather name this now than discover it in a post-game. Mitigation: newest-by-mtime + the self-declared session ID/timestamp in the header (contract element 1). **But the hazard is real, it is new, and I am introducing it.**

## 5. The migration path

**Non-destructive, idempotent, per-repo, requires no coordination:**

```
for each bare-name memento <persona>.md in io/mementos/ :
    cp  <persona>.md  <persona>-legacy-<mtime>.md      # COPY, never move
```

- **Copy, not move** — deliberately. A rename is a destructive-adjacent operation, and some bare slots are *actively pointed at* by rehydrate instructions living in other sessions' contexts right now. A copy cannot lose anything: if it fails, the original is untouched.
- After the copy, **every bare slot has an immutable twin**, and the bare slot may be overwritten harmlessly forever after.
- **Idempotent** — re-running it is a no-op (the twin already exists).
- The repo-root `.claude-memento.md` gets the same treatment: `cp .claude-memento.md .claude-memento-legacy-<mtime>.md`.

New writes use the session-ID name from day one. **No flag day, no coordination, no window during which the fleet is half-migrated and confused.**

## 8. Does this need Rick's ruling on (b)? — **YES, but not the ruling Sam framed**

Sam reserved option (b) — un-gitignoring `io/mementos/` — for Rick, because it **changes what a memento IS**: candid self-criticism about the work, the crew, and the human becomes permanent, committed, shipping repo evidence. **He is right to reserve it, and right about the cost.** I want to sharpen *why* the cost is worse than it first looks:

> **A memento that will be committed is a memento that will be written more carefully — and therefore less honestly.**
> The candor *is* the value. Sam's own memento is the proof: it contains the sentence *"I destroyed a record last night believing my own memento's line."* **That sentence is the most valuable thing in this entire incident, and it is exactly the kind of sentence that does not get written into a file the author knows will be committed, reviewed, and shipped forever.** Option (b) would tax precisely the thing we most need.

**So: my mechanism does NOT need (b) for the overwrite bug.** Overwrite becomes structurally impossible without any git involvement. On the question as filed, I can spare Rick the ruling.

**But I found a second bug, and it re-opens the question on different ground (§0-B).** `git clean -xdf` destroys every gitignored memento in a repo. Naming cannot help. Only *durability* can — and (b) *would* deliver it, since tracked files are not cleaned.

**Which is why I want to put a THIRD option in front of Rick that was on nobody's menu — Sam's (e):**

> ### (e) Separate the two bugs. Solve durability OUTSIDE the repo, so candor is never the price.
>
> **Mirror each memento to `~/.claude/mementos/<repo>/<persona>-<session_id>.md` on write.**
>
> - **Immune to `git clean -xdf`** — it is not in the repo.
> - **Immune to the gitignore question entirely** — it is not in the repo.
> - **Never ships, never gets committed, never gets reviewed** → **candor fully preserved.**
> - Survives `rm -rf io/`, a worktree teardown, a bad reset.
> - Machine-local — which is *correct*, not a limitation: a memento is about a session **on this machine**. It has never needed to travel with a clone.
>
> **This dominates (b) on both axes it was supposed to win on** — it gets *more* durability (git-clean-proof, not just recoverable) at *zero* candor cost.

| Option | Overwrite | `git clean` / durability | Candor |

| Naming + **(b)** un-gitignore | ✅ impossible | ⚠️ recoverable | ❌ **taxed** |
